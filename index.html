L2 Conversion & Storage

A 24-hour dispatch of solar, wind and other non-fossil power against demand. Storage soaks up the midday surplus and covers the evening peak. Surplus beyond storage goes to hydrogen, then is curtailed.

Hourly solar, wind and demand shapes, other non-fossil output, efficiency, durations and emission factor are illustrative assumptions. The 1.4 GWh BESS start value is a placeholder. Full list in the Assumptions view.

L3 + L4 Grid Intelligence & End-use

L3 Grid intelligence

Smart meters trim the evening peak. Better forecasts cut the fossil backup held in reserve. V2G adds a small flexible battery. Results feed straight into the L2 dispatch.

V2G only helps when there is surplus to charge from, so try it with the 2030 preset and millions of vehicles. At the 1,000-vehicle pilot scale the effect is negligible. All L3 values are illustrative.

    Assumptions, formulas and limits

    Everything the app assumes, in one place. Numbers in bold are read live from data.json.

    1. Model type

    The dispatch engine is a deterministic, transparent balance model. Given the same inputs it always returns the same outputs.

    2. Core equations

    1. E = P × CF × 8760 annual energy (P in GW, E in GWh)
    2. R(t) = P_solar × p_solar(t) + P_wind × p_wind(t) hourly renewable output
    3. R(t) + other(t) + discharge(t) + unserved(t) = D(t) + charge(t) + hydrogen(t) + curtailed(t) hourly energy balance. This version puts unserved energy on the supply side, which is where the engine counts it. The report's Section 3.3 shows it on the demand side; correct that line in the report.
    4. 0 ≤ SoC(t) ≤ S_capacity ; |charge(t)|, |discharge(t)| ≤ P_max storage limits, with round-trip efficiency applied
    5. CO₂e avoided = E_clean × EF_grid

    Other relations used

    • Effective peak = peak × (1 − 0.0005 × AMI %). Forecast reserve = unserved energy × 0.005 × forecast error %.
    • V2G energy (MWh) = EVs × kWh × available % × DoD / 1000; power = energy over the 4 h window (18:00–21:59).
    • Hydrogen (kt/day) = electrolyser GWh / kWh per kg. Rooftop units = kW × sun hours × 365 × PR.

    3. Illustrative assumptions (all tagged illustrative)

    • Solar profile: capacity × 0.75 × sin(π × (h − 6) / 12) for hours 6 to 18, zero otherwise.
    • Wind profile: capacity × (0.25 + 0.10 × cos(2π × (h − 2) / 24)).
    • Other non-fossil generation: 70 GW at 55% load factor.
    • Storage round-trip efficiency: 85%.
    • BESS duration: 4 h. Pumped-storage duration: 8 h.
    • Grid emission factor in the dispatch engine: 0.8 tCO2/MWh. Rooftop calculator: 0.71 kg CO2/kWh (unconfirmed).
    • Peak demand: 250 GW by default, with peak about 1.2 times the average.
    • Demand curve shape: a cosine peaking near 19:30. It is illustrative and not calibrated to any state or day.
    • Grid intelligence: 0.5% peak cut per 10% AMI coverage; 5% extra fossil gap per 10% forecast error.
    • L4 factors (cooking, diesel and hydrogen savings) and the firming sandbox factor are illustrative.

    5. Limitations

    • The model is not a forecasting tool.
    • It omits transmission limits, seasonal variation, ramp constraints and all cost elements.
    • The calculators omit degradation, discount rates and detailed site losses.
    • Social-media figures are sample-based and approximate.
    • The 1.4 GWh BESS start value is a placeholder from the PLI manufacturing snapshot, not installed grid storage.
    • L4 results are fuel savings; they do not subtract the extra electricity used by EVs, e-cooking and electrolysers. V2G recharge energy is not modelled separately.
    • The 2026 timeline card is a placeholder until a primary source is added.

    6. How to read the outputs

    Results should be read as transparent scenario illustrations, not as predictions or investment-grade analyses.
